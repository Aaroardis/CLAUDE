# PART ONE
# The Three Levels of Open

In my private sessions I often talk about Main Course Poly and Dessert Poly — because "open relationship" is a phrase that gets used for at least three completely different lives, and the confusion causes a lot of unnecessary heartache.

So let me lay all three out for you.

These are not stages, and nobody graduates from one into the next. They're three distinct architectures for love, and the right one for you is simply the one that fits how you genuinely want to live — not the one that sounds most impressive at a dinner party.

Read all three, and notice which one makes your chest open.

❖

## LEVEL ONE
## Dessert: The Cherry on Top

**One anchor bond, with play as delight added.**

There's a partnership at the center — a marriage, a nesting relationship, a life built together — and that bond isn't in question. Around it, occasionally, there's play: a threesome on an anniversary trip, a night at a club when you travel, a long flirtation that finally gets to go somewhere with everyone informed and everyone delighted.

For people living this way, non-monogamy is innocent fun, or a naughty night out, or a bit of shared mischief that reminds you both that you're alive and desirable and lucky. It's the cherry on top of a serious partnership, and it's not meant to be the foundation.

**What it tends to look like:**

● Play happens together, or with full knowledge and real enthusiasm on both sides
● Frequency is occasional, and often tied to specific settings — travel, parties, events
● Outside connections stay erotic and warm rather than romantic and ongoing
● The daily rhythms of the anchor relationship don't change much at all
● Agreements can stay fairly simple, because the footprint is small

**What makes it work:**

Genuine enthusiasm, rather than reluctant permission.

Dessert Poly runs beautifully when it's FUN for both of you, and when a "not this time" is received with exactly the same ease as a yes.

> **Occasional is not lesser. A twice-a-year adventure that keeps a fifteen-year marriage erotically awake is a masterpiece of relationship design.**

❖

## LEVEL TWO
## Part of the Picture

**Non-monogamy as a real, ongoing part of the relationship — carefully designed not to disturb it.**

Here, openness has moved out of the "special occasion" category and become a genuine part of the relationship's life. It's discussed. It's alive. It's a real feature of who you are together, rather than something that only surfaces on an anniversary trip.

And — this is the crucial part — it's deliberately built so that it doesn't disturb the essential function of the bond.

There's no shared calendar full of other people's names. There are no standing weekly lovers, no second date nights, no restructuring of home life. What there IS: known comets — the lovers who orbit back around every so often, familiar and welcome, without ever becoming a fixture. Some light agreements rather than an elaborate architecture. Active, honest dialogue that's simply part of how you two talk. And sometimes a don't ask, don't tell arrangement, when that's what genuinely brings both of you peace.

The essential function of your bond might be happy adoration — the plain pleasure of being one another's favorite person. Or it might be a consistent, peaceful home that's holding children, aging parents, a business, a health journey, or hard-won calm. Whatever it is, you know what it is, and you're protecting it on purpose.

**What it tends to look like:**

● Known comets who reappear now and then, rather than regular or standing lovers
● Light agreements — a few clear ones, not an elaborate rulebook
● Active dialogue, where this stays a living conversation instead of a one-time permission
● Sometimes don't ask, don't tell — when BOTH people truly want it
● No shared calendars, no new weekly rhythms, no rearranging of the household

**What makes it work:**

Knowing exactly what you're protecting, and saying it out loud. When you can both name the essential function of your bond — *this home stays peaceful* or *we stay each other's favorite* — then every agreement you make has an obvious job, and you stop arguing about rules and start protecting something you actually love.

And a note on don't ask, don't tell, because it gets a bad reputation it doesn't always deserve: it's a beautiful, workable choice when both people genuinely prefer it and neither is quietly using it to avoid something. Ask yourselves honestly which one is happening. Then revisit it now and then, because the answer can change.

> **Part of the Picture is a deliberate design choice — you're expanding what's possible between you, without asking your bond to become something other than what it already does beautifully.**

❖

## LEVEL THREE
## Foundational ENM: The Main Course

**Non-monogamy as the architecture itself — and very often, as identity.**

For some people, polyamory is the foundation of the self, and THUS the foundation of every relationship they build. It's philosophically meaningful to them, deeply aligned with their values, emotionally rich, and core to their sexual expression.

Here there may be no primary partner at all, or there may be several loves of comparable weight, or there may be a whole constellation that functions as home in a way that no single pairing ever could. And for many people living this way, non-monogamy isn't something they're trying out — it's who they have always been, from the moment they first understood their own heart.

This is the Village. The Collective. The kitchen table with too many chairs, the group thread that plans the holidays, the network that arrives with food when somebody's parent gets sick.

**What it tends to look like:**

● Multiple loves held as equally significant, or a structure that deliberately refuses ranking altogether
● Ongoing partners with continuity and history — names on the shared calendar, date nights that aren't with each other
● Deep interconnection, where partners of partners are family rather than strangers
● Community as the container, rather than a couple with visitors
● Identity language — this is who I am, not a thing I'm doing
● Very high communication skill, because the system is genuinely complex and deserves care

**What makes it work:**

Honesty about your real bandwidth, and a true love of the collective. Foundational ENM asks the most of you, and what it gives back is specific and rare: a life in which you are known by many people, and in which love is abundant, distributed, and remarkably resilient.

It's also the oldest arrangement in this playbook. Humans lived in interdependent, multi-bonded communities for most of our history, and polyamory is ancient and ancestral — societies in which women have led have consistently experienced greater collaboration and greater peace. The isolated pair as the entire structure of adult life is the recent experiment, not this.

> **Foundational ENM isn't a relationship with extra people in it. It's a different answer to the question of what love is FOR.**

❖

## All Three Are Beautiful… And So Is Monogamy

Let me be very clear here, because this is precisely where shame likes to sneak in.

Dessert is not beginner poly. Part of the Picture is not a compromise anybody settled for. Foundational ENM is not the advanced level that the serious people eventually reach.

They are three complete, worthy, well-designed lives — and monogamy is a fourth, when it's chosen consciously by someone who considered the alternatives and picked the one they actually wanted, with the same care they'd bring to any other decision about how to spend their life. Monogamy by conscious choice is a gorgeous structure. Monogamy by default is simply the instruction sheet somebody handed you before you were old enough to read it.

Marriage, monogamy, monogamish-ness, ENM, polyamory, solo-poly, demisexuality, asexuality, and the relationships that support LGBTQ+ identities — these are ALL beautiful, valid, valuable parts of the same revolution.

The goal was never to be as open as possible. The goal is to be as ACCURATE as possible.

And you're allowed to move! Seasons change, capacity changes, desires change, and love changes. Moving from one level to another isn't a failure and it isn't a promotion — it's design, responding honestly to a real life.

# PART TWO
# The Luck of Living Right Now

## The Templates You Inherited Weren't Built for Your Delight

For most of recorded history, partnership was administered rather than designed.

We are emerging from a transactional, heteronormative, Judeo-Christian marriage model in which society was managed through property transfer, power consolidation, and the control of women's sexuality and fertility through fear, shame, and law. Marriage was the mechanism by which property moved, families consolidated influence, and lineage stayed traceable.

Those structures worked quite well for what they were built to solve.

But they were never solving for long-term delight. They were never solving for a woman's expression, exploration, or pleasure. They were never solving for two people staying erotically alive across four decades together, and they certainly weren't solving for the possibility that a person's desires might legitimately transform at fifty.

Not one of the people who drew up those blueprints was consulting you.

> **The old templates aren't wrong because they're old. They're limited because they were built to answer an entirely different question.**

❖

## What's Actually Happening Now

As far as I see it, we are experiencing — right now — a revolution in modern relationships. And we are the revolutionaries.

Older structures are being examined out loud, in public, at scale, and there's an explosion of polyamory, ENM, and open relating happening across cultures all at once. This reflects something much deeper than a lifestyle trend: it reflects people who have become people who desire growth, learning, experience, enjoyment, abundant personal rights, freedom, and thriving. These longings extend far beyond basic safety — they're the exploration of our highest human potential.

A few things worth noting:

**(1)** This is all wonderful.
**(2)** This is hard, because revolution and evolution require enormous effort and vulnerability.
**(3)** Without the structure of precedent there's a void — a blank canvas — and that can be genuinely confusing.
**(4)** And we get to fill that blank canvas with conscious, inclusive, and beneficial choices.

Mononormativity — the assumption that one partner, forever, exclusively, is the natural and superior form of love — operates socially AND internally, quietly shaping what we believe is acceptable, available, and possible for us. Naming it out loud is most of the work of getting free of it, because once you can see an assumption AS an assumption, you finally get to decide whether you actually agree with it.

❖

## And Isn't It Lucky…

Here's the part I'd love you to sit with for a moment.

You happen to be alive at the exact moment in human history when it became possible to build love from scratch — not to reform the old model, not to sneak around its edges in secret, but to sit down with the people you love and consciously design something that has never existed before, with everyone's informed and delighted consent.

Relationships that include all of this, out in the OPEN? It hasn't happened before. Not like this.

That's an extraordinarily rare opportunity, and most people who ever lived did not get it.

You have it.

> **This isn't just personal evolution. This is cultural revolution — and the way you love is shaping the future.**

# PART THREE
# Radical Transparency

## The Skill That Makes Every Level Work

If you take only one thing from this playbook, please take this one.

Radical Transparency is the practice of telling the whole truth — early, warmly, and without quietly editing it down into whatever you've decided the other person can handle. It's the highest-leverage skill available in any relationship, and in open relationships it isn't a nice quality to have, it's structural. Everything else in this playbook rests on it.

Not honesty as a moral position. Honesty as the thing that holds the building up.

> **A heart that can speak total truth — and either stay unshaken, or shake a little and come home to calm — is relational gold.**

Please read that second half again, because it's the part almost nobody says out loud.

❖

## You Don't Have to Be Unshakable

There's a fantasy floating around that the truly evolved person hears absolutely anything without flinching — that their partner describes a magnificent night with somebody else and they feel nothing at all but warm, immediate, uncomplicated generosity.

Some people really are like that, and it's lovely.

Most of us shake a little. And here's the good news, which I'd like you to hold onto: shaking a little and returning to calm is mastery too — arguably the more useful kind, because it's actually available to real humans with real nervous systems and real histories.

The skill was never the absence of reaction. The skill is the RETURN.

➢ You hear something that lands somewhere in your body
➢ You notice the shake — heat in the chest, tightness in the throat, the urge to say something sharp
➢ You stay in the room, and you keep breathing
➢ You let the feeling move through you, and you let it be information rather than a verdict
➢ You come back to steady

That's the entire capacity, and it's completely trainable. It gets faster with practice, and it's worth more than any agreement the two of you could ever write down.

❖

## Transparency Has Two Halves

**Speaking it.** Saying the true thing before you've workshopped it into something more palatable — the desire you're not certain is acceptable, the attraction that genuinely surprised you, the thing that happened last weekend that you'd very much prefer to summarize.

**Receiving it.** Building enough security within yourself — rooted in your own values and your own sense of worth — that when somebody brings you their honesty, they aren't punished for having brought it.

Both halves are required. A relationship where one person tells the truth and the other person makes truth expensive isn't a transparent relationship — it's a trap with excellent branding.

> **Every time truth is met with warmth instead of consequence, you make the next truth easier to tell.**

❖

## Their Informed Consent Requires… You Informing

Let me give you a healthy challenge here.

Tell people exactly what you can provide, and exactly what you cannot.

This is hard! Why? Because if you're fully honest you might lose an opportunity for connection. And because you want to be liked and well-thought-of. And because sex and sensual connection are beautiful and nobody enjoys missing out.

BUT — nobody can give informed consent to sex or to a sexual relationship without truthful and complete information about how they'll be cared for or not, how their body will be tended to or not, how their heart will be engaged or not, and how their daily life will be supported through ongoing romantic connection… or not.

Here's the very good news: **you can be SUAVE AF while being totally transparent.**

It sounds something like this:

*"I want to kiss you, touch you, and fulfill your wild fantasies this weekend. I'm free for flirty text messages this week to keep your body at an erotic hum. I'm not aligned with monogamy or romantic partnership right now. I can't offer daily support or emotional caretaking — but if that's what you want, I'm genuinely happy for you to have it elsewhere. Tell me your desires and your limits, and let's see whether there's a real overlap. I engage with clear boundaries, and I'd love for you to as well."*

The key is to avoid the messy situationships that exist ONLY because one person isn't revealing their authentic needs, and the other isn't revealing their authentic boundaries.

> **There can be no "meeting of minds" where there is no clarity.**

❖

## Some Practical Rules of Radical Transparency

**Tell it early.** The truth told beforehand is a plan. That same truth told afterward is a confession. Same words, entirely different relationship.

**Lead with the headline.** Don't build toward it across eleven minutes of careful context — say the thing, and then give the context. The slow build is almost always for your own comfort rather than theirs.

**Know the difference between transparency and unloading.** Radical Transparency means telling your partner what they need in order to consent, participate, and stay close to you. It doesn't mean narrating every passing attraction in exhaustive detail because your discomfort feels lighter once it has become their problem. Ask yourself honestly: is this for our connection, or is this me handing off a feeling?

**Ask what detail they actually want.** Some people want the whole picture, warmly and specifically. Others want the headline and nothing more. Both are completely legitimate, and the answer changes over time, so ask again.

**Let the wobble be welcome.** When you're bringing something tender, make room for the reaction: *"I want to tell you something, and I want you to be able to have whatever response you have."* And then let them have it, without rushing them back toward fine.

**Repair quickly when you miss.** You'll edit something. You'll sit on something for a week. When you notice, simply name it — *"I've been holding this and I want to tell you why"* — because that isn't a failure, that's the practice working exactly as it should.

# PART FOUR
# The Foundations

There are some things it's worth thinking about carefully, whichever level you're living. Let's go through them.

❖

## It's a Good Idea to Think About Your Motivation

For some people polyamory feels natural and even simple — it just makes room to love who they love, whenever it feels right. Others are drawn toward novelty, or kink, or sexual exploration, or the fuller expression of an LGBTQ+ identity. Others are stepping around relationship agreements that had begun to feel confining. Others are born rebels who don't much like social rules — and others absolutely LOVE the clear structure and explicit agreements that polyamory can provide! Others want long-term stability with a co-parent alongside romance and sex outside the partnership. And others are after inclusive, loving constellations of co-parents, where community itself is the goal.

All of these are workable. What matters is that you can name yours out loud.

**So… what's your motivation?**

❖

## It's a Good Idea to Get Secure Within Yourself

Polyamory pushes us right up against our insecurities and attachment wounds — it's confronting! It can inspire immense growth, and it also asks for a deep personal sense of security, so that new exposures and new dynamics don't destabilize love that matters enormously to you.

Build your own steadiness first, in whatever way works: aligned friendships, mastering a skill you care about, meditation, therapy, movement, spiritual practice, or simply spending regular time being fully honest with yourself about how you feel.

❖

## It's a Good Idea to Build Security With Each Other

With polyamory there's often a great deal of emphasis on choice, because there are simply more options and experiences on the table. So if we choose a sexy adventure or an intimate romance with somebody new… how does our partner know that our connection is secure?

We need to know why they choose us, and for what. Which means we need clear agreements, named priorities, rituals and habits, healthy communication practices, and real emotional reassurance — especially inside a long-term project like a marriage, or a home, or welcoming new life.

❖

## It's a Good Idea to Think About Bandwidth

**Love is limitless. Time is not.**

So who and what will you prioritize, and when? Do you honestly have the emotional bandwidth, the free time, the head space, and the energetic room in this chapter of your life for more love or more lovers?

Some things worth watching:

● Protect specific, non-negotiable time with each person who matters to you
● Give somebody your BEST hours sometimes, not only whatever remains after work and travel
● Notice which relationship keeps quietly absorbing all of the cancellations
● Remember that rest counts, and that overscheduling yourself across three people serves nobody at all

❖

## It's a Good Idea to Start With Vision, Not Rules

Most open relationships begin with a list of restrictions — no sleepovers, no kissing, nobody we already know. But restrictions are attempts to prevent a feared outcome, which means a rulebook is really a fear list wearing a very nice suit.

Start somewhere warmer. Start with what you're building toward, and ask each other:

➢ What do we want MORE of in our life together?
➢ What does each of us want to explore, and what is it that draws us there?
➢ What kind of people do we want around us and around our home?
➢ How do we want to FEEL at the end of a night when one of us has been out?
➢ What does a really great version of this look like three years from now?

Agreements come afterward, and they come as things that serve the vision — rather than as a fence built around a fear.

❖

## It's a Good Idea to Treat Agreements as Living Documents

An agreement made in month one, by two people who had never done any of this before, does not get to govern year four.

● Write your agreements down, because memory is remarkably generous toward whoever benefits
● Put a review date on them — quarterly is plenty
● Notice which agreements you'd happily renegotiate and which ones are load-bearing
● Changing an agreement together is completely normal. Quietly breaking one is the problem

And when you need the relationship to shift, adjust the agreements explicitly, out loud, together. Remember: **branches that bend don't break.**

❖

## It's a Good Idea to Get Curious About Jealousy

Jealousy is not a moral failure, and it isn't evidence that any of this is wrong for you. It's a signal — and signals can be read.

Underneath jealousy there is almost always something quite specific:

➢ *I want more time with you* — which is a logistics problem, and those are easily solved
➢ *I want to feel chosen* — which is a reassurance problem, solved with words and attention
➢ *I'm afraid of being replaced* — which is a security problem, solved slowly and consistently
➢ *I want what you're having* — which is desire, wearing a disguise
➢ *Something is genuinely off here* — because sometimes jealousy is simply accurate, and that deserves listening to as well

The work was never to eliminate jealousy. The work is to get curious quickly enough that it becomes useful information instead of an argument.

❖

## A Note on Compersion

Compersion is the pleasure you feel at your partner's pleasure with somebody else.

It's real, it's available, and it is absolutely not a requirement. Some people feel it immediately and easily. Some feel it after years. And some never feel much of it at all, and run gorgeous open relationships anyway on the strength of trust and goodwill and genuine care.

If it arrives, drink it in — it's one of the loveliest experiences in this whole territory. And if it doesn't, you haven't failed at a single thing.

❖

## It's a Good Idea to Handle Safer Sex Like Adults

Unsexy conversations create very sexy freedom. Have them.

● Agree on barriers, testing frequency, and what gets disclosed to whom
● Have that conversation BEFOREHAND, rather than in the moment with somebody new
● Name what happens if something changes, because occasionally something will
● Update the agreement as your network naturally shifts

❖

## It's a Good Idea to Name Your Hierarchy Honestly

Some structures have a primary partner who carries more weight in decisions. Some are deliberately non-hierarchical. Both work beautifully.

What doesn't work is claiming there's no hierarchy while quietly running one. If a partner has veto power, or gets the holidays, or comes first when two things collide — say so, warmly and clearly, to everyone involved. People can happily consent to a clear structure. Nobody can consent to a hidden one.

❖

## It's a Good Idea to Think About Transparency… Everywhere Else

If you're in partnership and you have other lovers or partners, are you going to be open with each other? With friends, family, children, schools, colleagues at work? Will you share broad strokes or details? What will you share with your lovers about your primary partner? Will your kids know what you do and with whom, or meet the others you might come to love?

Whatever you decide is beautiful. Just decide it together, and revisit it as your life changes.

❖

## And Finally… Build the Village

The very best open relationships aren't simply partner-expanding — they're community-building.

You're recreating something ancient and precious here: an interdependent web of people who know each other, who care for each other, and who reliably show up. Metamours who become real friends. A partner's partner at your birthday dinner. A network that surrounds you when something hard happens, so that no single person has to carry all of it alone.

That, honestly, is the gift underneath the whole structure — and it's the part people fall in love with.

> **Polyamory and ENM at their best aren't about having more partners. They're about belonging to more people.**

# PART FIVE
# Why This Is Relational Empowerment

Four things happen when you design your love life on purpose.

## Your Pleasure Becomes Intelligence

Pleasure is not indulgent — it's intelligent. It's a source of truth, self-knowledge, creativity, energy, and even transcendent wisdom, and it will tell you exactly what you want, what you're ready to release, and where you've quietly been performing.

When you stop managing your desire and start listening to it, your entire relationship with yourself begins to change.

## Presence Replaces Performance

Open relating asks you to say what's actually real — what you want, what you need, what frightened you last Thursday — and that requirement builds something remarkable in a person: you gradually stop editing yourself down into somebody easier to be around.

You get to be real, unfiltered, wild, raw, and sacred… and still be chosen.

## Autonomy Lives Inside Connection

Love stops requiring you to merge, dissolve, or hand over the parts of yourself that were never anybody else's to hold in the first place.

Two — or three, or four, or more — sovereign people, choosing to relate from a place of authenticity, without giving up who they are. What's yours stays yours, and what's shared is shared entirely on purpose.

## The Structure Can Actually Hold Your Evolution

You will change. Your desires at fifty won't match your desires at thirty, and thank goodness for that.

A well-designed relationship treats your growth as a shared value rather than a threat. Stability stops meaning sameness day after day, and starts meaning something far better: a stable orientation toward each other, with communication and compassion, especially in times of growth.

> **This is what Relational Empowerment produces — a love life spacious enough and strong enough to hold your freedom, your aliveness, and your evolution.**

# PART SIX
# Your Practices

## One… The Design Inventory

Take out a journal, find a quiet half hour, and answer these honestly — without editing for how the answers sound.

➢ What do I actually want more of in my erotic life?
➢ What am I curious about that I've never once said out loud?
➢ Which of my current relationship agreements did I truly choose, and which did I simply absorb?
➢ Where am I still conforming, rather than choosing?
➢ What would I build if there were no template at all?

❖

## Two… Which Level Am I Genuinely Wanting?

Read the three levels again, and this time notice your BODY rather than your opinion.

Where does your chest open? Where does your stomach tighten? Which one makes you feel expansive, and which one makes you feel like you'd be performing something for somebody?

Then check the second layer, gently: is this the level I want, or the level I think I should want? Both directions count — wanting less openness than you imagine you're supposed to want is just as real and just as valid as wanting more.

❖

## Three… The Opening Conversation

For bringing this to a partner. Choose a calm moment rather than a charged one — never right after sex, and never right after a fight.

**Start with the relationship, not the request:**
*"I love what we have, and I want it to keep getting better for a very long time."*

**Name the curiosity, not the demand:**
*"I've been thinking about how we might design more freedom into what we've built, and I want to explore that WITH you."*

**Give them room:**
*"I'm not asking for a decision today. I'm asking to have the conversation, over time, with all of your real reactions welcome."*

**Then ask, and actually listen:**
*"What comes up for you when I say that?"*

And let it be the first of many conversations, rather than the only one.

❖

## Four… The Jealousy Inquiry

The next time it arrives, don't argue with it. Investigate it.

**1.** Locate it. Where is it living in your body — chest, throat, gut, jaw? Put a hand there.
**2.** Breathe into it. Five full minutes, even ten. Don't fix it, and don't explain it. Just feel it, and let it move.
**3.** Name what's underneath. Time? Reassurance? Security? Desire? Or something that's genuinely off?
**4.** Make one specific request. Not *"I need you to be different,"* but something like *"I want Saturday mornings to be ours, always."*

Most jealousy shrinks dramatically once it's been felt all the way through and translated into a real, sayable ask.

❖

## Five… The Weekly Sit-Down

Twenty minutes, the same day each week — and not because something's wrong. ESPECIALLY when nothing is.

● What's coming up on the calendar that involves other people?
● What felt really good this week?
● Is there one small thing that landed funny that I haven't said yet?
● Has either of us been sitting on something?
● What do we want more of next week?

The relationships that thrive inside open structures are almost always the ones that talk while things are still lovely.

# In Closing…

You now have the three levels, the one skill that makes all of them work, the foundations that keep the whole thing joyful, and some practices to begin with tonight.

What you build from here is entirely yours.

Design a love life that answers your real desires. Tell the truth earlier than is comfortable. Let your heart shake, and let it come home to calm. And treat every person involved — including yourself — as a full human being with a full inner life and a heart that deserves care.

Then notice what happens around you as you do it, because the way you love changes the people you love, and the people they love, and the rooms you all walk into. Every relationship built on honesty and delight makes the next one a little more possible for somebody who's watching.

Be gracious and patient with yourself. This is revolutionary relational work, and it's new work, and it hasn't been done quite this way before.

> **The future of love is more free, more diverse, more custom-made and co-created — less about assumption and replication, and far more authentic to the selves involved. You're not waiting for it. You're building it.**
