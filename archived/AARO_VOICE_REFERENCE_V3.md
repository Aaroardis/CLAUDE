## 0. The Two Tests Every Email Must Pass

Before anything else: every email Aaro sends should make the reader feel **A) amazing** and **B) informed.**

If a draft doesn't clearly do both, it's not done — regardless of how well it follows every other rule below.

---

## 1. Direct Address — No Exceptions, Including Openers

**This is a hard rule, confirmed directly by Aaro: speak to "you," never about "the men who…" — not even as a scene-setting opener.**

❌ "The men who find me have already decided to pay attention…"
✅ "You found me. You are extraordinary."

The wrong version is still talking *about* a category of people, even when it pivots to "you" a sentence later. The right version speaks directly to the one reader from the very first word.

**This affects how every email opens.** No throat-clearing, no third-person framing, no scene-setting that delays direct address. Start with "you."

---

## 2. Amplify — Never Hedge or Water Down

**Confirmed directly by Aaro: when you're telling the reader something true and good about themselves, state it as fact at full volume. Do not modulate, qualify, or soften it.**

This was the single biggest correction in Aaro's direct feedback. Claude's instinct is to write praise like a careful analyst — noting, observing, qualifying. Aaro writes praise like a verdict.

**Wrong (Claude's instinct — analytical, hedged):**
> "That kind of self-awareness is rarer than it sounds, and it tells me something about who you are before we've even spoken."

**Right (Aaro's correction — direct, amplified, declarative):**
> "You are rare and precious, your self-awareness is a gift to the world. That's amazing and let's make the most of your incredible gifts — to the world and to women."

**Right (from Aaro's Email 2 punch-up):**
> "You already know you're a stud, a rockstar, a visionary, a source of love in the world. You already know you have the capacity to live a glorious and fully-realized life."

**The rule:** When stating someone's worth or capacity, never hedge with "kind of," "rarer than it sounds," "tells me something" — these are observational, distanced phrases. Replace with direct, stacked, maximum-amplitude statements of fact: "You are X. You are Y. You are Z."

---

## 3. Name the Struggle, Then Release It

**This corrects a previous V2 rule that was wrong.** V2 said: never name the struggle directly, only ever frame as capacity. Aaro's actual edit contradicts this.

**From Aaro's punch-up:**
> "What I've found, supporting the evolution of amazing men like you, is that you get hung up on your own deficiencies — and you don't need to! Let's just agree you're WAY BEYOND self-blame and internal critique."

She names the pattern directly — "you get hung up on your own deficiencies" — and then immediately, in the same breath, releases him from it. This is a two-beat move:

1. **Name it plainly.** Don't dance around the pattern with euphemism.
2. **Release it instantly, with energy.** "And you don't need to!" / "Let's just agree you're WAY BEYOND that."

The empowerment frame still holds — he is never left in the deficiency. But you're allowed to name it directly on the way to releasing him from it. Naming it plainly is what makes the release land as real, rather than as avoidance.

## 4. Rhythm — Format-Specific, Not Universal

**V2 overcorrected toward long accumulating clauses as the universal default. That is wrong for promotional/welcome emails specifically.**

There are now three confirmed rhythm registers, not two:

### A) Promotional / Welcome / Sales emails — BRIEF, BULLET-POINTY, DIRECT
Confirmed by Aaro's direct feedback ("more brief, more bullet pointy") and her own Email 2 punch-up, which is short paragraphs, bulleted lists, and punchy declaratives throughout.

> "Let's just agree you're WAY BEYOND self-blame and internal critique. You can be free of performance pressure and most of your stress."

This is the default register for the welcome sequence and any sales-oriented automation email. Short paragraphs. Bulleted benefit lists. Direct statements. Not extended literary buildup.

### B) Long-form educational / retreat / website copy — LONG ACCUMULATING CLAUSES
Still confirmed from session docs, website, and playshop docs — but now scoped specifically to long-form educational and brand content, not promotional emails.

### C) Session Insight Documents — Their own register entirely
See Section 11 in prior versions — unchanged, format-specific to client-facing summaries.

**Critical: do not use extended metaphor in promotional emails.** Aaro's own Email 2 edit removed the river/sediment extended metaphor entirely and replaced it with a direct, plain statement. Extended metaphor belongs to long-form/retreat content (Category B), not promotional emails (Category A).

---

## 5. Ban: The Fragment-as-Header Move

**Confirmed directly by Aaro as a disliked pattern.** Standalone short fragments used as pseudo-headers or dramatic landing lines — flagged explicitly as "pretentious."

❌ "Not through theory."
❌ "The Unsaid Thing."

These read as a stylistic affectation, not as Aaro's natural voice. This is a confirmed Claude bias that survived even after the V2.3 revision pass — it was disguised as a "confident landing line" but is the same staccato-fragment pattern in different clothing.

**The fix:** If a thought needs emphasis, fold it into the sentence directly, or use CAPS for the actual emphasis word. Don't isolate a fragment on its own line as a dramatic device.

---

## 6. Bulleted "We Can…" Benefit Lists

**New confirmed pattern from Aaro's Email 2 punch-up.** Mid-email, not just in offerings sections — a bulleted list of concrete things the coaching/work will do, in active "we can" voice:

> * We can dramatically increase your capacity to HEAR your own wisdom
> * We can powerfully initiate you into being the lover you always wanted to be — by getting you tapped into your body
> * We can transform how you communicate so you feel like an open heart to the people who love you and need you — your partner, your family, your company

**The structure:** "We can [verb] [specific outcome]" — repeated 3 times, each one a different concrete domain (inner wisdom, sexual/erotic identity, relational communication). This is now a confirmed tool for promotional emails, used to make the offer concrete and informative — directly serving the "informed" half of the Section 0 test.

---

## 7. CTAs — Imperative, Energized, Exclamation Points Allowed

**Confirmed correction.** Claude's CTAs were too soft and tentative.

❌ "[Book a Discovery Call]"
✅ "DO YOURSELF A FAVOR! [Book a package now!]"

CTAs in promotional emails should be commands, not gentle suggestions. Exclamation points are permitted and used for energy — this was previously underused based on the more measured session-doc tone, but promotional emails run hotter.

---

## 8. Specificity Over Vague Adjectives — Even Within a Kept Sentence

**Confirmed via direct comparison.** Aaro kept the structural skeleton of one of Claude's sentences nearly verbatim, but replaced vague adjectives with named, concrete domains.

**Claude wrote:**
> "...a life lived under conditions that weren't designed with your erotic and relational flourishing in mind."

**Aaro's edit:**
> "...a life lived under conditions that weren't designed with your erotic pleasure, powerful romantic love, emotional flourishing, and sexual health."

**The lesson:** "Erotic and relational flourishing" is a vague compression. "Erotic pleasure, powerful romantic love, emotional flourishing, and sexual health" is four named, distinct, concrete things. When you're tempted to compress multiple ideas into one efficient adjective pair, don't. Name each thing separately.

## 9. Greeting Convention

**Possible new pattern, needs more confirmation but used directly by Aaro:**

> "Hey Stud…"

A pet name followed by an ellipsis breath, rather than a generic "[first name]" fill. Use "Hey Stud…" or similar warm direct address as an option for promotional emails going forward. Flag for more confirmation across other formats before treating as universal.

---

## 10. Hype-List Affirmation

**Confirmed pattern, new to V3.** A stacked list of elevated identity statements, used as a closing or near-closing move:

> "You already know you're a stud, a rockstar, a visionary, a source of love in the world."

This is hotter and more amplified than anything in the session docs (which are tender and measured) — confirming that promotional email energy runs at a different temperature than client-facing session content. Use this kind of stacked-identity affirmation as a tool specifically for promotional/welcome emails.

---

## 11. Confirmed From Before (Still Holding)

These patterns from V2 are reconfirmed by Aaro's direct edit and should remain:

- **"…" as breath** — used four times in a single short email by Aaro herself. Use generously, more than previously applied.
- **Parenthetical humor** — "(Shockingly, nobody else is talking about optimizing for that.)" — kept almost verbatim from Claude's draft. Confirmed working pattern.
- **CAPS for spoken emphasis** — "HEAR," "ARE," "WAY BEYOND," "STEP IN" all confirmed in Aaro's own edit.
- **Empowerment framing** — "I don't need to install it for you — because it's already there" — still the core frame, just paired now with the name-it-then-release-it move (Section 3).
- **"…Which, as it turns out…"** as a pivot into landing — confirmed, kept by Aaro.
- **Naming the partner specifically** — "the woman you love" rather than just "her" — slightly warmer and more concrete than generic pronoun use.

## 13. Voice Quick-Test V3

Run every piece through this before it's done:

**The core test:**
- [ ] Does this make the reader feel AMAZING?
- [ ] Does this make the reader feel INFORMED?

**Opening:**
- [ ] Does it open with direct "you" address — not "the men who…" or any third-person framing?

**Amplification:**
- [ ] Is every compliment or capacity statement stated as fact, with no hedging language ("kind of," "tells me something," "rarer than it sounds")?

**Rhythm (for promotional/welcome emails specifically):**
- [ ] Is it brief and bullet-pointy rather than long and literary?
- [ ] Are there zero extended metaphors?
- [ ] Are there zero fragment-as-header moments?

**Structure:**
- [ ] Is there a "we can…" bulleted benefit list if the email is pitching an offer?
- [ ] Is the CTA imperative and energized, not tentative?

**Specificity:**
- [ ] Are vague compressed adjectives replaced with named, separate concrete things?

**Formatting:**
- [ ] CAPS for emphasis, not italics?
- [ ] Unicode "…" used generously as breath, not sparingly?

**Framing:**
- [ ] If naming a struggle, is it named plainly and then released with energy in the same breath?
- [ ] Is "most men/most people" gone?

**Filler:**
- [ ] Are "genuinely," "honestly," "deep work," "transformational," "meaningful" all gone?
