## Neo Tantra

If you hear "Tantra" and think of erotic massage, eye-gazing, or polarity practices, you're thinking of modern NeoTantra — a movement that originally emerged in the early 1900s and gained momentum through teachers like Pierre Bernard, who started the Tantrik Order of America in 1905, and most famously Osho, who had some access to limited Tantric practices but was not part of a lineage teaching traditional Tantra. He often created his own modern methods combining Western therapeutic processes and meditation.

NeoTantra tends to focus on Sacred Sexuality, Relationships, and "Tantric Sex" at its center. At times it has merged with the New Age movement and even neo-Christianity in the context of heavily gendered polarity teachings, which have little to nothing to do with traditional Tantra — since traditional Tantra was a transgressive path that emphasized breaking out of cultural narratives and boxes. NeoTantra often contains a lot of cathartic and ecstatic practices, energy work, sex magic, and manifestation practices, alongside therapeutic, holistic elements. These kinds of practices can fall under a category called Bhoga in the traditional sense — about enjoying our lives and experiencing more pleasure and empowerment.

There's often a perception that NeoTantra is purely sex-focused, but most reputable schools recognize that how we make love and connect with our eroticism isn't just about sexual technique — it's about how we relate to our emotions, boundaries, body, and more. Much of NeoTantra emphasizes the energy body and experiencing intimacy beyond the physical: becoming more sensitive, emotionally mature, and mindful about our lives and relationships. Most involve rituals, meditation, and yogic techniques. You're just as likely to see people crying and doing intensive shadow work in a NeoTantra workshop as you are to see people touching each other and orgasming.
