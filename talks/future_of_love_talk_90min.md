# PART ONE — CONTEXT
### Where we all started, and where we actually are
**(20 min)**

## Talking points

● Nearly all of us grew up inside a heteronormative, mononormative society
● Mononormativity = monogamy assumed as the standard, without question
● It shapes what we believe about love long before we get to think it through for ourselves
● You arrived into adult life carrying assumptions: who you're permitted to want, how many people you're allowed to love, what it means about you if your desires don't fit
● And here's the truth of it — we happen to be alive in a genuinely magical time and place
● More freedom and more voice than people have ever had to decide what happens in our love lives, our sex lives, our long-term partnerships, our families
● The isolated pair as the entire structure of adult life is the RECENT experiment
● Humans lived in interdependent, multi-bonded communities for most of our history
● This isn't a moment to be a bypasser — this is a moment for each of us to take the lead

## Quick vocabulary (fast, 3 min)

● **Open relationship** — committed partnership, reads as monogamous outside, sexual play with others
● **ENM** — umbrella term, everyone knowingly agrees to non-exclusivity, leans sexual in everyday use
● **Polyamory** — love of many, multiple romantic emotionally-engaged relationships, full knowledge and consent

## Group questions

➢ What did you inherit about love that you never actually chose?
➢ Whose relationship did you grow up watching — and what did it teach you was possible?
➢ What's one assumption about love you've already let go of?

**Interactive:** pairs, 4 minutes. Trade one inherited rule you're done with.

# PART TWO — CHOICE AS REVOLUTIONARY
### Three styles, and the freedom to move
**(20 min)**

## Talking points

● Three broad styles of open relating — all beautiful, all valid, all worth building a life around
● **The Cherry on Top** — one anchor bond, play as delight added. The bond isn't in question
● **Part of the Picture** — real, ongoing, deliberately designed NOT to disturb the bond
● **Foundational ENM** — non-monogamy as the architecture itself, and very often as an identity
● Point of Foundational might be community. Or it might be total freedom and autonomy — Solo Poly, primary orientation to self
● YES, you can be poly without a relationship and without a primary. It's about orientation and desire
● Occasional is not a beginner level or a lesser choice
● A twice-a-year adventure that keeps a fifteen-year marriage erotically awake is a masterpiece of relationship design
● You don't have to pick one and commit to it forever — plenty of us float between them
● Monogamy chosen consciously, with care and curiosity, is every bit as beautiful
● Marriage, monogamish-ness, ENM, polyamory, solo-poly, demisexuality, asexuality, LGBTQ+ relationships — ALL valuable parts of the same revolution
● **Love is limitless. Time is not.**

## Group questions

➢ Which style is calling you right now — and which one were you taught to want?
➢ What's your actual motivation? Novelty, kink, queerness, community, freedom, more of yourself being fed?
➢ Do you have the time? The emotional energy for curiosity, challenge, and repair?
➢ Can you offer every person involved a rich experience?

**Interactive:** silent hands, eyes closed — Cherry on Top / Part of the Picture / Foundational / still deciding. Then open the room: what surprised you about your own hand?

# PART THREE — THE BRAVERY OF TRANSPARENCY
### The skill everything else rests on
**(25 min)**

## Talking points

● In open relationships transparency isn't a nice quality to have. It's STRUCTURAL
● Radical Transparency = telling the whole truth, early, warmly, without quietly editing it down into whatever you've decided the other person can handle
● **Truth told beforehand is a plan. That same truth told afterward is a confession.** Same words, entirely different relationship

## Transparency has two halves

● **Speaking it** — boldly naming what excites you, what feels daunting, which parts of you are sitting in confusion. Trusting your voice to be clear and true even while you're being watched. Disclosing facts and feelings without shame… and maybe even with pride
● **Receiving it** — welcoming someone's truth as neutral information about another independent being on their own journey, rather than as evidence they've done something wrong by failing to please you
● It's a beautiful thing to cultivate both halves

## You don't have to be unshakable

● Most of us shake a little
● Shaking a little and returning to calm is mastery too — arguably the more useful kind
● You hear something… it lands a little hard in your body
● You notice the shake — heat in the chest, tightness in the throat, the urge to say something sharp
● You stay in the room, you keep breathing
● You let it move through you, and let it be information rather than a verdict
● You come back to steady… then curious… then open
● Completely trainable. Gets faster with practice

## Informed consent — what to agree on together

● **Sexual health** — testing history, frequency, barriers, what you use with whom. Agreed BEFORE, never in the moment
● **What's on the table?** — kissing, numbers, texting, dates, love. One by one, because you may have very different pictures in your heads
● **What happens afterward?** — how much you each want to know, who tells whom, when
● **What's the long-term vision?** — six months, three years
● This is hard! Why? Because if you're fully honest you might hear a no. Because you want to be liked. Because you don't want to hurt someone or be rejected for your truth
● Tell them anyway. Generously, and in advance
● Sexual safety and consent are beautiful trust builders — not a hurdle before the good part. They ARE part of the good part

## Group questions

➢ What's the truth you've been holding — and what would it take to say it early instead of late?
➢ When someone brings you honesty, what happens in your body first?
➢ What do you actually want to know? Whole picture, or headline only?
➢ Where do you edit yourself down into something easier to be around?

**Interactive:** pairs, 6 minutes. One person speaks a real desire out loud with no softening. Partner's only job is to receive it as neutral information. Swap. Debrief the room on what it felt like to RECEIVE.

# PART FOUR — SECURITY IN DYNAMISM
### Building structure that can hold your evolution
**(15 min)**

## Talking points

● Start with vision, not rules
● Most open relationships begin with a list of restrictions — but restrictions are attempts to prevent a feared outcome
● Start warmer. What do we want MORE of? What do we want to explore? What kind of people do we want around our home? How do we want to FEEL at the end of a night?
● Agreements come afterward, as things that serve the vision — rather than as a fence built around a fear
● **Security within yourself** — polyamory pushes right up against our insecurities and attachment wounds. It's confronting, and it can inspire immense growth
● **Security with each other** — we need to know why they choose us, and for what. Clear agreements, named priorities, rituals, real emotional reassurance
● **Jealousy is not a moral failure** — it emerges from insecurity, and insecurity is one of the most human things there is
● It can come from wondering whether we really have stability and longevity here
● It can come from a sense of personal deficiency, or from prior experiences with infidelity
● The invitation: make room for what feels imperfect, hold it without judgment, witness it with clear eyes as part of the human dance of being full of bliss and desire and love… and also full of confusions and insecurities
● Get curious as fast as you can. Jealousy felt all the way through and named out loud becomes neutral information — and turns into tender opportunities for care
● **Compersion** — real, available, and absolutely not a requirement
● **Name your hierarchy honestly** — people can happily consent to a clear structure. Nobody can consent to a hidden one
● **Agreements are living documents** — write them down, put a review date on them. Changing one together is completely normal
● Structure is strength and order, in a territory that can bring you sweet freedom and wonderful wildness
● Your relationship needs BOTH for there to be beautiful evolution — the order that holds you steady, and the wildness that keeps you alive

## Group questions

➢ What does security actually feel like in your body — and who taught you that?
➢ What's the reassurance you've never asked for out loud?
➢ Where is jealousy pointing you right now?
➢ If you named your hierarchy honestly today, what would you have to say?

**Interactive:** journal 4 minutes, then popcorn share — one line each. "The agreement I most want to make is…"

# CLOSE (5 min)

● You will change, and so will your partner
● A skillfully built and well-held structure holds all of that — freedom, stability, and change
● Whatever you decide is beautiful. Just decide it together, and revisit it as your life changes
● Be gracious and patient with yourselves — this is revolutionary relational work, and it hasn't been done quite this way before
● You are here on earth to be joyfully alive, loving, and loved — so boldly build whatever relational architecture you need to hold your evolving heart

**Final ask:** one word from each person on how they're leaving the room.
